# Evaluation seed

This is an incomplete development-only seed, not a representative or independently adjudicated evaluation corpus. It contains 54 historical human-source excerpts and six assistant-authored AI examples. The two command-line excerpts are translations of the same document and are correlated, not independent. The AI examples are related variants of a single scenario, not independent samples. Their prompt records are post-hoc task descriptions, not captured requests; they are not counted as adversarial tests. The regression gate uses this exact provisional seed. Do not tune rules or thresholds against this seed alone. There is no held-out test set yet.

The English historical excerpts `1962-computers` and `declaration-1776` are public domain in the United States; rights elsewhere may differ. The Indonesian encyclopedia excerpts credit Wikipedia contributors under CC BY-SA 4.0. The English documentation, legal and opinion excerpts retain source-specific MIT or Apache notices, or CC BY/CC BY-SA license copies, as recorded in the manifest. The Global Voices excerpt credits Oiwan Lam and translator Vinna Florencia in its attribution frontmatter and uses CC BY 3.0; a license copy is in `LICENSES/CC-BY-3.0.txt`. Its live publication and modification dates are not immutable archival evidence. The eight 2010–2020 news excerpts credit Wikinews contributors and are distributed under CC BY 2.5; a license copy is in `LICENSES/CC-BY-2.5.txt`. The English and Indonesian command-line excerpts credit Joshua Levy and the listed authors and translators, and are distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0. The Sastrawi excerpt retains the MIT notice in `LICENSES/SASTRAWI-LICENSE.txt`. The Indonesian legal text is attributed to the specified Wikisource revision; its underlying legal-text status is documented in the manifest, with the transcription subject to Wikisource terms. The Bahasa Indonesia and Bediding excerpts credit Wikipedia contributors and their 2019 revisions; the coding-interview excerpt credits John Washam and the translation's contributors at its 2020 commit. All three use CC BY-SA 4.0; the license text is in `LICENSES/CC-BY-SA-4.0.txt`. Original retrieval dates before this seed are unknown; the earlier entries' `retrievedDate` records copying from the repository's existing corpus on 2026-11-04.

The Indonesian PHP tutorial credits Risan Bagja Pradana and retains its source MIT notice in `LICENSES/id-koding-php-tutorial-2018-MIT.txt`; the English Open Source Guides excerpt credits its contributors under CC BY 4.0. The OpenTelemetry excerpt credits its contributors and includes its Apache 2.0 license in `LICENSES/en-opentelemetry-overview-2020-APACHE-2.0.txt`. All three cite the exact source revision in the manifest.

Before further tuning, assign a deterministic split stratified by language, label and genre, retain source and license evidence, and freeze a genuinely unseen held-out set. T3's provisional structural change was made before a holdout existed; reserve genuinely unseen sources for final validation. Do not label agent-authored text as human. Samples require 150–600 words; the seed counts use whitespace-separated words. The first 500 words of the Sastrawi document were selected in source order with whitespace normalized.

Evaluation JSON includes exact development input texts and manifest metadata as `devCorpus`. The regression check refuses missing or different development inputs rather than comparing incomparable aggregates. Corpus validation rejects identical text across dev and test after whitespace normalization; it does not detect paraphrases, shared sources, translations, or other related samples. The provisional baseline is intentionally rebuilt when the development seed changes, with the reason in the changelog; a complete corpus and frozen holdout are still needed. Decoded input text, including line endings, must match the baseline; the check fails closed if they do not.
